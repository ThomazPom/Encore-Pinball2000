# Prototypes and development history

The prototype record is useful when it explains a production decision, but it
is unusually vulnerable to mislabeled photographs and memories detached from
dates. This topic therefore keeps observation and recollection separate.

## Development path

The preserved accounts broadly agree on this sequence:

1. experiments with reflected CRT imagery demonstrated the “holographic” idea;
2. the Pinball 2000 team converted that concept into a serviceable, modular
   production platform;
3. Revenge From Mars and Star Wars Episode I shipped in 1999;
4. additional games, most visibly Wizard Blocks, were under development when
   Williams closed the pinball division.

George Gomez's [making-of transcript](../sources/pinball2000de-making-of/README.md)
is first-person evidence for the product concept. The newer
[development-lore series](../sources/pinball2000-development-lore-part-1/README.md)
adds a programmer's view of the software team's formation and constraints.

> [!NOTE]
> A prototype page proves that the pictured or described object existed in that
> state. It does not by itself establish a final ruleset, planned production
> date or the exact status of every similarly named build.

## Durable research lead

The Strong Museum of Play finding aid lists internal Williams folders titled
“Pinball 2000,” “P2000 Issues,” and “Revenge from Mars,” containing items such as
memos, service logs, change notices, bills of materials and test-location
records. These holdings are not mirrored here; they are catalogued as a future
on-site or reproduction-request research lead.
